A **[portfolio](term:portfolio)** is a small collection of your best projects. Before your first developer job, **your projects are your experience**: they prove you can build things, not just follow tutorials.

## Quality over quantity

Three finished, polished projects beat fifteen half-done tutorial copies. A strong portfolio project:

- **Solves a real problem**, ideally one you or someone you know has.
- **Is deployed**: there's a live link to click ([Deployment](/roadmap/deployment)).
- **Has a great README**: what it does, a screenshot, how to run it, and what you learned ([Documentation](/roadmap/documentation)).
- **Has clean history**: small, described Git commits show how you work.
- **Shows range**: for example a Python script/tool, a web app with a backend, and something with data.

## DevPath itself counts!

You've watched DevPath being built commit by commit, and you'll be changing it yourself. Once it's on GitHub, it's a real React + TypeScript project with tests. Be honest in its README about which parts you built.

## Your GitHub profile

- **Pin** your best repositories.
- Add a **profile README**: a short intro, what you're learning, and links.
- A real name and photo build trust.
