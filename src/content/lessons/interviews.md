Developer interviews usually have a few parts. Each is a skill you can practice, and practice is what makes the difference.

## The typical stages

1. **Recruiter call**: a friendly chat about you and the role.
2. **Coding challenge**: solve a problem live or at home, often [algorithms](/roadmap/algorithms) and [data structures](/roadmap/data-structures).
3. **Technical questions**: "What's the difference between a list and a tuple?", "What happens when you type a URL?"
4. **Behavioral interview**: "Tell me about a time you…": how you work with people and handle problems.

## Coding challenges: think out loud

Interviewers care about **how you think** as much as the answer:

1. **Repeat the problem** in your own words and ask questions. "Can the list be empty?"
2. **Work through an example** by hand.
3. **Describe a simple approach first**, even a slow one, and its [Big-O](/roadmap/big-o).
4. **Write the code**, talking as you go.
5. **Test it** with your example and edge cases.
6. **Improve it** if there's time.

Being stuck is normal. Saying what you're thinking lets the interviewer help you.

## Behavioral questions: the STAR method

Answer with a short story:
- **S**ituation: the context.
- **T**ask: what you had to do.
- **A**ction: what *you* did (say "I", not "we").
- **R**esult: what happened, and what you learned.

Prepare 5–6 stories: a hard bug you fixed, a time you learned something fast, a disagreement, a mistake you made.

## Key takeaways

- Expect a coding challenge, technical questions and behavioral questions.
- In coding challenges: clarify → example → simple solution → code → test, all **out loud**.
- Use **STAR** for behavioral answers, and prepare your stories in advance.
